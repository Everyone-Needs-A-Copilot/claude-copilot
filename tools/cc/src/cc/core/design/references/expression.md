# Deliberate emphasis and reduction

For bolder work, identify the one important idea that lacks emphasis, then choose
a deliberate composition, scale, contrast or medium that makes it clearer. Increase
expression only where the surface's job and approved design direction warrant it;
do not decorate all components equally or replace an operational interface's
familiar controls with novelty.

For quieter work, identify competing focal points and reduce their visual weight
while preserving hierarchy, status, affordances and accessibility. Removing every
accent can erase the primary action and make a surface harder to use.

For distillation, remove a step, decision, container or repeated message only when
the user can still find the content, complete the task and recover from errors.
Preserve necessary disclosure, provenance and domain detail. Test a real journey
before and after; a smaller screenshot is not proof of a simpler experience.

Use product-specific content and real assets; generated illustrations must not
pretend to document a real outcome or customer. Respect any explicit constraints
on imagery, fonts, language and file scope. Record the chosen direction and the
alternative it improves on without promoting that choice into personal taste or
organization policy. Hand QA comparable captures and required behavior checks.
