# Moments That Matter

<!--
FACILITATION GUIDE — Service Designer
======================================
Moments That Matter are the highest-stress struggling moments in the journey: the actions where the experience misses what someone expected and blocks their progress hardest. They are where the product either earns trust or loses it. They are NOT features.

HOW THEY ARE SELECTED (do not pick by gut feel):
- Start from the journey map (20-journey-maps.md). It is told from one primary stakeholder's point of view, from their trigger, with a progress statement ("When [situation], I want to [progress], so I can [outcome]", plus why) for every stakeholder.
- Only STRUGGLING moments qualify: an action where the experience missed the person's expectation. A circumstance or reality ("not enough clients") is never a moment that matters; at most it is the trigger.
- Each struggling moment has a STRESS score 1-10 (10 = most intense pain when progress is blocked) with cited evidence.
- The moments that matter are the highest-stress struggling moments: exactly 3 when the journey has fewer than 12 moments, exactly 5 when it has 12 or more.
- Stress selects the set. How often a moment happens and how easy it is to fix may decide which one to design FIRST; they never swap a lower-stress moment into the set.
- If a moment that matters is where someone quits and restarts elsewhere (a loop), say what they carry into the next attempt.

For each moment, define both the B- state (mediocre, what the product might deliver if we're not careful) and the A+ state (excellent, what it should actually feel like). The gap between B- and A+ is the design challenge.

PREREQUISITE: Journey maps (with struggling moments, stress scores, forces and the moments that matter selected) and JTBD must be completed.

CONVERSATION FLOW:
1. Confirm the moments that matter from the journey map (3 or 5, highest stress, evidence cited)
2. For each, restate the expectation, what missed, the stress score, the forces per stakeholder, and why it matters
3. Define the B- state (mediocre) and A+ state (excellent)
4. Define the design implications (what the product must do here)
5. Capture pain-to-delight transformations
6. Rank by stress; sequence design work by frequency and addressability

QUESTIONS TO ASK:

## Round 1: Confirming the Moments
- "From the journey map: how many moments are there? So we need [3 / 5] moments that matter."
- "Which struggling moments have the highest stress scores? What evidence backs each score?"
- "Is any of these really a reality rather than an action that went wrong? If so, it's the trigger, not a moment that matters."
- "Is there a struggling moment with a higher stress score that we left out? Why?" (If none can justify it, it goes in.)
- "Is there a moment where AI-generated content is first shown to the user? Where does it rank on stress?"

## Round 2: Why It Matters, Per Stakeholder
For each moment that matters:
- "What did they expect? What happened instead?"
- "What progress does this block, and for whom?"
- "What does it cost when it goes wrong: the quit, the switch, the lost referral?"
- For each stakeholder present: "What's pushing them, pulling them, worrying them, and what habit keeps them stuck here?"

## Round 3: B- to A+ Analysis
B- (mediocre):
- "What does this moment look like if we build it adequately? What does 'good enough' feel like for the user?"
- "What's the version of this moment that works but doesn't delight?"

A+ (excellent):
- "What does this moment look like if we build it exceptionally? What does the user say, feel, or do differently?"
- "What's the version of this moment that earns a word-of-mouth referral?"
- "What stress score would this moment have in the A+ state?"

For each A+ state, ask:
- "What capabilities enable this?"

## Round 4: Design Implications
For each moment:
- "What must the product do at this moment to earn trust?"
- "Which force does the design have to beat: their anxiety, or their habit?"
- "What information must be visible?"
- "What must the AI get right?"

## Round 5: Pain-to-Delight Transformations
- "What are the recurring pain points in the current process that this product inverts?"
- "For each pain point: What is the current state? What is the A+ state?"

## Round 6: Ranking and Sequence
- Rank the moments that matter by stress (#1 = highest). Ties: the one with stronger evidence ranks higher; say so.
- "Which of these do we design for first?" Frequency and how addressable the moment is can set the sequence.
- "Which moments are minimum lovable product anchors — the ones the initial release must nail?"

SYNTHESIS:
Present as named, numbered moments (MTM-1 is the highest stress) with:
- A vivid scene description (time, context, who is there)
- Expectation, what missed, stress score and evidence
- Forces per stakeholder
- Why it matters
- Success and failure states
- The B- to A+ narrative (current state → excellent state)
- What capabilities enable the A+ state
- Design implication

Follow with the Pain-to-Delight table, the MLP anchors and the design sequence. MTM-4 and MTM-5 are used only when the journey has 12 or more moments.
-->
